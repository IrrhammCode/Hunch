# F3 — How short-horizon binary / UP-DOWN markets are framed in consumer trading products

**Angle:** Polymarket, Kalshi, Aevo, Deribit, Nadex, Robinhood — prediction markets vs binary options vs event markets vs vanilla crypto options. How apps label these features inside a broader trading product. How to present Hunch’s 60s BTC UP/DOWN CPMM market as a flagship **Markets** (not TradFi equity options) module.

**Date of research:** 2026-09-18  
**Method:** WebFetch of Wikipedia product/regulatory pages, CFTC investor alert, Aevo docs. Direct product marketing sites (Polymarket, Kalshi, Deribit) often blocked by transport; framing inferred from regulatory filings language, secondary sources, and official docs where reachable.

**Brief assumption locked in:** Hunch “option” feature = short-horizon UP/DOWN (binary) markets with CPMM YES/NO, **not** TradFi equity options.

---

## 1. Taxonomy used by serious product teams

| Product type | Payoff | How consumer apps label it | Typical venue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vanilla options (equity/crypto) | Right, not obligation, to buy/sell at strike | **“Options”** (tabs, chains, strikes) | Robinhood Options, Deribit, Aevo, CBOE |
| Binary / digital / fixed-return | All-or-nothing cash settle on yes/no | **Avoided** by regulated retail platforms; called **binary options** in fraud/enforcement literature | Offshore brokers; Nadex still uses “binary options” for regulated DCM product |
| Event / prediction contracts | $0–$1 (or $0/$1) on event outcome | **“Event contracts,” “Prediction markets,” “Markets,” Yes/No** | Kalshi, Polymarket, Robinhood Prediction Markets Hub |
| Short-horizon crypto UP/DOWN | Binary on BTC direction over 60s etc. | Market / Predict / Up-Down — **not** “options” when CPMM YES/NO | Closest analog: event markets + crypto binary products |

## 2. Prediction markets (Polymarket, Kalshi) do **not** brand as “options”

### Item 2.1 — Academic/common form is binary, but product name is “prediction market”

- **Claim:** Industry and press call Polymarket/Kalshi **prediction markets** or **event contracts**, not options, even though the dominant contract is a binary 0–1 event.
- **Quote:** “**Prediction markets**, also known as **betting markets**, **information markets**, **decision markets**, **idea futures**, or **event derivatives**, are open markets that enable the prediction of specific outcomes using financial incentives. … The most common form of a prediction market is a **binary option** market, which will expire at the price of 0 or 100%.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prediction_market
- **Date:** Wikipedia page as of research fetch 2026-09-18 (revision context mid-2026)
- **Confidence:** High — this is definitional language widely reused in product/regulatory copy.

**Implication for Hunch:** Mechanics can be binary while the **nav label** is still “Markets” / “Event Markets.” Do not inherit the Wikipedia academic synonym “binary option” into UI.

### Item 2.2 — Kalshi’s public identity is a prediction / event-contract platform

- **Claim:** Kalshi is framed as a CFTC-designated **event-contract / prediction market** platform, not an options exchange. Wikipedia’s current lead even labels it an “American online prediction gambling company,” reflecting how regulators and media treat the category.
- **Quote:** “**Kalshi Inc.** is a **prediction market** platform based in Manhattan, New York City … Individuals can place bets on other future outcomes, including economic indicators, weather patterns, awards, political and legislative outcomes, and military conflicts.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalshi
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High on product framing; Medium on “gambling company” descriptor as marketing truth (it is third-party characterization).

### Item 2.3 — Kalshi / CFTC language: event contracts + rulebooks + Yes/No resolution

- **Claim:** Regulated US framing is **event contracts** / designated contract markets under the CEA, with Yes/No resolution against source agencies — not strike/expiry option chains.
- **Quote:** “Every single Kalshi contract is bound by a rulebook filed with the CFTC, which identifies authoritative data sources (Source Agencies) and a Payout Criterion for a ‘Yes’ resolution. … settling each winning contract with a payment of one dollar while all the other contracts expire worthless. Kalshi operates as a designated contract market under the Commodity Exchange Act.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prediction_market#Resolution_and_settlement_of_event_contracts
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High.

### Item 2.4 — Polymarket markets as crypto prediction markets (Yes/No shares)

- **Claim:** Polymarket positions itself as a cryptocurrency-based **prediction market** where users trade outcome shares; sports dominate volume but are **not** labeled a sportsbook or options product in primary identity language.
- **Quote:** “**Polymarket** is an American cryptocurrency-based **prediction market** which offers a platform where individuals can place bets on future outcomes … trade shares that represent the likelihood of specific future outcomes.” “While it is not labelled a sports betting platform, 63% of trades on the platform are for sports bets.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymarket
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High on labeling; Medium on current volume percentages (secondary stats).

### Item 2.5 — Price = implied probability, YES/NO CLOB/CPMM — UI pattern: “Yes / No,” not “Call / Put”

- **Claim:** Prediction-market UI copy is probability + Yes/No, not strike/greeks.
- **Quote:** “Each event will have a ‘Yes’ or ‘No’ tradable contract. For example, if a ‘Yes’ contract around an event occurring has a market price of $0.93 then the market is implying that there is a 93% that this event will take place.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prediction_market#General_mechanics
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High.

**Hunch mapping:** CPMM YES/NO on “BTC up in 60s” is **this** product family. Flagship CTAs should be **Up / Down** or **Yes / No** with a market list — not strike pickers.

### Item 2.6 — Robinhood super-app pattern: separate “event contracts” from equity “options”

- **Claim:** Major retail apps that already sell TradFi **options** deliberately **segment** prediction/event contracts as a different product surface (Robinhood lists event contracts alongside stocks, options, futures, crypto — as distinct categories; Kalshi is infrastructure for Robinhood’s Prediction Markets Hub).
- **Quote:** “It provides an electronic trading platform that facilitates trades of stocks, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), **options**, index options, futures contracts, **event contracts on prediction markets**, and cryptocurrency.” “In 2025, Kalshi became the infrastructure for Robinhood’s **Prediction Markets Hub event contracts**.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinhood_Markets  
  (also Kalshi wiki for hub quote)
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High.

**Implication for Hunch:** Best-in-class pattern for a **trading super-app** is: one nav with **Markets** (or **Event Markets / Predict**) **separate** from any future vanilla **Options** tab. Do not merge 60s UP/DOWN into a tab named Options if you also want TradFi credibility later.

### Item 2.7 — Kalshi expansion into perpetual futures still uses derivatives labels distinct from event markets

- **Claim:** Even when Kalshi moves toward financial derivatives, media distinguish **perpetual futures** from **event contracts**; they do not rebrand events as “options.”
- **Quote:** “The company launched federally regulated perpetual futures products tied initially to cryptocurrency markets … raising questions about leverage, retail investor protection, and regulatory boundaries between **event contracts and traditional futures products**.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalshi#History
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** Medium-High (summary of 2026 expansion coverage).

## 3. What “options” means in vanilla product UIs (Deribit, Aevo)

### Item 3.1 — Finance “option” = right but not obligation at a strike

- **Claim:** Consumer and professional **Options** modules imply exercise rights, strike prices, expiry, and premium — not all-or-nothing CPMM tickets.
- **Quote:** “an **option** is a contract which conveys to its owner, the **holder**, the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell a specific quantity of an underlying asset or instrument at a specified strike price on or before a specified date.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_(finance)
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High.

### Item 3.2 — Aevo docs: “Options” = ETH/BTC (and alts) vanilla-style derivatives product

- **Claim:** Crypto DEXes that ship an **Options** product mean exchange-traded options on underlyings with orderbooks and margin — not 60s UP/DOWN event contracts.
- **Quote:** “Aevo is a decentralized derivatives exchange built on a custom OP Stack Layer 2. **Options**, perpetual futures, OTC, and automated strategies, all from a single margin account, with off-chain order matching and on-chain settlement.” “Perpetual futures and **options on ETH, BTC**, and a growing list of altcoins, with cross-margin support and sub-10ms latency.”
- **URL:** https://docs.aevo.xyz/ (docs readme) — https://www.aevo.xyz/docs/readme.md
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High (official docs).

### Item 3.3 — Aevo PERPS+ still packages “options” as defined-risk structured payoffs, not binary events

- **Claim:** Even “options made easy” consumer wrappers keep the **options** label for structured/vanilla risk, not yes/no markets.
- **Quote:** “Aevo PERPS+ Combine perpetuals and options into a single trade. PERPS+ packages directional exposure with **defined-risk protection**, giving you structured payoffs without managing multiple legs manually.”
- **URL:** https://www.aevo.xyz/docs/readme.md → PERPS+ overview
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High on docs language.

### Item 3.4 — Deribit

- **Claim:** Deribit is a crypto derivatives venue known for **options** (European-style BTC/ETH options) and perpetuals; direct deribit.com marketing pages were not reachable this research pass (transport error / 404 on Wikipedia). Product taxonomy in industry coverage remains “options” = strike/expiry products.
- **Quote:** (Unavailable this pass — cite only as known-venue category, not as a direct quote.)
- **URL:** https://www.deribit.com/ (fetch failed 2026-09-18); no Wikipedia article at /wiki/Deribit
- **Date:** 2026-09-18
- **Confidence:** Medium — directional only; do not invent Deribit UI strings.

**Implication for Hunch:** If judges or users have seen Deribit/Aevo/Robinhood **Options**, a bare Hunch **Options** tab creates **false product class**. They will expect chains, strikes, or at least exercise mechanics you do not have.

## 4. Binary options: regulated status and why the label is toxic

### Item 4.1 — Binary option ≠ vanilla option; all-or-nothing; no right to the underlying

- **Claim:** Binary options are exotic all-or-nothing contracts; they do **not** convey the right to buy/sell the underlying.
- **Quote:** “A **binary option** is a financial exotic option in which the payoff is either some fixed monetary amount or nothing at all. … also called **all-or-nothing options**, **digital options** … and **fixed return options (FROs)**.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_option
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High.

### Item 4.2 — CFTC/SEC investor alert: binary options + short-horizon yes/no + platform fraud

- **Claim:** US regulators publish dedicated fraud alerts for **binary options** platforms; the yes/no price-path structure (including short clocks) is the exact public stereotype regulators attach to scams.
- **Quote:** “A binary option is a type of options contract in which the payout will depend entirely on the outcome of a **yes/no proposition**. The yes/no proposition typically relates to whether the price of a particular asset … will rise above or fall below a specified amount.” “When the binary option expires, the option holder will receive either a pre-determined amount of cash or nothing at all.” “…manipulation of the binary options trading software to generate losing trades … the countdown to expiration is extended arbitrarily until the trade becomes a loss.”
- **URL:** https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/fraudadv_binaryoptions.html
- **Date:** CFTC/SEC joint investor alert (undated on page; long-standing policy page, retrieved 2026-09-18)
- **Confidence:** High on content; Medium on publication date.

**Hunch risk:** A 60s BTC UP/DOWN product marketed as “options” or “binary options” will be compared to this enforcement literature, not to Deribit.

### Item 4.3 — Retail bans: ESMA, ASIC, Israel, and many jurisdictions

- **Claim:** Retail **binary options** are banned or tightly restricted in major jurisdictions; calling a consumer feature “binary options” imports that ban surface by association.
- **Quote:** “The European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) has banned retail binary options trading.” “ASIC considers binary options as a ‘high-risk’ and ‘unpredictable’ investment option, and finally also banned binary options sale to retail investors in 2021.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_option#Regulation_and_fraud
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High.

### Item 4.4 — Prediction markets themselves are treated as gambling in many places — even without the word “options”

- **Claim:** Calling the module **Markets** does not eliminate regulatory risk; Polymarket/Kalshi are blocked or sued as unlicensed gambling in multiple countries/states.
- **Quote:** “Singapore’s Gambling Regulatory Authority blocked Polymarket as an illegal online gambling platform in December 2024.” “Prediction markets are classified as **betting platforms** in the United Kingdom, and require operating licenses from the Gambling Commission.” Spain banned Kalshi and Polymarket for operating without a gambling license (May 2026).
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prediction_market#Legality_and_regulation ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymarket
- **Date:** 2026-09-18 fetch
- **Confidence:** High.

### Item 4.5 — CFTC has a dedicated consumer education surface for prediction markets / event contracts

- **Claim:** US regulatory consumer navigation separates **“Understanding Prediction Markets and Event Contracts”** from binary-options fraud alerts — a public taxonomy Hunch can mirror.
- **Quote:** Learn & Protect navigation includes: “Understanding Prediction Markets and Event Contracts” alongside the binary-options fraud alert page.
- **URL:** https://www.cftc.gov/LearnandProtect/PredictionMarkets (nav seen on binary-options alert page 2026-09-18)
- **Date:** 2026-09-18
- **Confidence:** Medium-High (nav observed; page body not fully re-fetched).

## 5. How apps label the feature **inside** a broader trading product

### Observed patterns (synthesis)

1. **Robinhood (broker super-app):** Product list is explicit and segmented — stocks, ETFs, **options**, index options, futures, **event contracts on prediction markets**, crypto. Prediction surface branded as hub/event contracts (via Kalshi), **not** folded into Options revenue/features copy as if they were the same.
2. **Kalshi (event-native):** Entire app is **markets / event contracts**. Sports >90% of activity, but corporate identity remains prediction/event-contract platform. Mansour quote emphasizes financialization of opinion, not “options trading.”
3. **Polymarket (crypto-native):** **Markets** / probability shares / Yes-No. Docs/marketing: markets “reflect real-time sentiment.” Never positioned as a crypto options chain.
4. **Aevo / Deribit (crypto derivatives-native):** **Options** tab = vanilla-style crypto options + perps under one margin account. Adding a 60s binary would likely live under a **different** product name (or not exist).
5. **Nadex / Crypto.com lineage:** Historical US retail binary exchange **does** use “binary options” as product name (HedgeStreet → Nadex); also offers knock-outs and call spreads — products that avoid pure binary branding.
6. **Hunch internal brand (brief):** Dark UI + lime + **UP/DOWN** — already closer to event-market / direction-market UI than options-chain UI.

### Recommended framing for Hunch (flagship module)

| Layer | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Top nav / flagship tab** | **Markets** (best) or **Event Markets** / **Predict** / **Up/Down** |
| **Module subtitle** | “Short-horizon onchain markets” · “60s BTC UP/DOWN” · “Onchain event markets” |
| **Trade CTA** | **Up** / **Down** (or **Yes** / **No**) + countdown + CPMM odds — not Call/Put + strike |
| **Docs / judge pitch** | “Flagship **Markets** module: CPMM binary event markets on BTC direction (60s), Pyth-resolved — event contracts, not TradFi equity options.” |
| **Internal code names** | Fine to keep `Market` / `HunchFactory` / short-horizon binary markets |
| **What to avoid in UI** | Bare tab **Options**; **Binary options**; **Digital options**; implied Greeks/strike/exercise; “equity options” analogies |

**If a judge slide must speak “options” energy:**  
Use a one-line disclaimer under the module: *“Onchain binary event markets (UP/DOWN) — not exchange-traded equity/crypto vanilla options.”*

**Why “Markets” beats “Options” for the pivot (P):**  
A multichain **trading app** with Portfolio + Trade (spot/perps) + **Markets** (60s events) + Swap reads as a complete IA. “Options” that are not options creates product-class confusion when the rest of the app is multichain trading.

## 6. Risks of calling Hunch’s feature “Options”

| Risk | Severity | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Product-class mismatch** — users/judges expect strike/expiry/exercise like Deribit/Aevo/Robinhood Options | High | Option (finance) definition; Aevo “options on ETH, BTC” |
| **Regulatory association with banned retail binary options** | High | ESMA/ASIC retail bans; CFTC/SEC binary-options fraud alert |
| **Gambling / prediction-market enforcement adjacency** (even under Markets label) | High–Medium | Polymarket/Kalshi bans, state AG suits, UK betting classification |
| **Misleading marketing** if copy implies TradFi options sophistication | Medium | CFTC alert on overstated ROI; binary ≠ right to underlying |
| **App store / judge trust** — hackathon judges may flag “fake options” if CPMM YES/NO is sold as Options module | Medium | Hunch brief lock: feature is not TradFi options |
| **Future vanilla options collision** — cannot later add real options without IA rewrite | Medium | Robinhood segments event contracts vs options |
| **Does not eliminate gambling risk** — renaming to Markets still faces jurisdiction risk for short-horizon bets | High | Prediction market legality section |

## 7. Decision support (F3 slice of P / R / S)

- **Naming does not block P** (multichain trading app + flagship short-horizon feature), but **the flagship must ship under Markets / Predict / Up-Down**, not Options.
- **S** (ship Hunch as-is on Monad): strongest if UI already says UP/DOWN and “markets”; avoid pivot-time rename to Options.
- **R** (reposition, stay single-chain): “mobile onchain markets / trading” language is **aligned** with Polymarket/Kalshi taxonomy and safer than “options app.”
- Evidence **against** a flagship tab literally named **Options** for CPMM 60s UP/DOWN is strong across prediction-market practice, vanilla-options product practice, and binary-options regulation.

## 9. Bottom line for parent agent

**Best naming:** Flagship module = **Markets** (alt: **Event Markets** / **Predict** / **Up/Down**), with mechanics copy “short-horizon binary event contracts / CPMM YES-NO on BTC direction.”  
**Do not** ship a bare **Options** tab for the 60s product.  
**Do not** use **Binary options** / **Digital options** in consumer UI.  
**Risks of “Options”:** product-class falsehood, ESMA/ASIC/CFTC binary-options stigma, gambling-enforcement adjacency, and judge/user distrust when the rest of a multichain trading app implies spot/perps/vanilla derivatives.
